# MANDATE-primary 2026Q2 demo evaluation: executive summary

**Author:** Lead Analyst
**Date:** 2026-06-04
**Status:** Pre-deposit demonstration evidence. Not formal Phase 6 evidence. PROTOCOL_LOCK §13 binds the formal study to the frozen `mandate-eval-primary-2026q2-v1` tag, which is unchanged by any work described here.

## What was done

Three real-world stakeholder specification requests were grounded in 36 real public-source documents (PDF, DOCX, PPTX, HTML), ingested through the apparatus's production extractors, and pushed end-to-end through MANDATE-primary's six fine-tuned role pipeline on the eval host's Ollama. The scenarios span two domains: security operations (Volt Typhoon CISO request, CrowdStrike outage CIO request) and financial reporting (Silicon Valley Bank collapse CFO request). Both the CrowdStrike and SVB requests explicitly demanded "three distinct strategic options." Every source binary's SHA-256 is recorded in `demo/<scenario>/sources/originals/ORIGINALS_MANIFEST.json`; every URL fetch is logged in `demo/<scenario>/sources/fetch_report.json` with its actual HTTP outcome. Each scenario was run twice through MANDATE-primary: once against web_fetch text-mode extracts, and once against fresh extracts produced by the apparatus's own `pypdf` / `python-docx` / `python-pptx` extractors. A direct single-prompt Claude Sonnet 4.6 baseline (the protocol's B1) was also run on the CrowdStrike request for comparison.

## What MANDATE-primary did

On all six Ollama runs (three scenarios × two extraction paths) the apparatus's primary integrity gates passed cleanly with one exception: every fine-tuned role fired (`llm_used=True`), no role silently fell back to the deterministic path (`any_llm_fallback=False`), and the Procedure-role's RAG retriever was correctly wired into each scenario's Jaccard index. Per-role temperatures held to the frozen `configs/llm_defaults.json`. The artifacts were schema-valid mandate-as-code with the full field set populated. The exception, on the SVB from-binaries run, is the substantive finding documented below: the Binding role emitted a structured refusal rather than a `decision_summary`, which the unpatched apparatus treats as a parse failure.

## Four substantive findings

**1. The Decomposition role currently produces exactly one COA per request, across two domains and both extraction paths.** Thirteen runs on record (three scenarios × four pipeline variants plus stub-adapter replays) all emitted `n_coas=1`, the same `COA-1: Minimal manual assessment approach` name, and the same two-node DAG (Manual Assessment → Results Documentation). The CIO and CFO requests explicitly demanded three options; Decomposition still emitted one. This is a structural prior in the fine-tune, not a scenario-dependent artifact, and not an apparatus defect. It is the principal training-data finding for the upstream MANDATE team.

**2. The Interpreter role's anchor distillation flips between two output modes depending on upstream chunk content, in both directions.** On CrowdStrike, the web_fetch run produced a cleanly structured anchor minimum ("Three distinct strategic options for endpoint security architecture with (a) operational and capex implications, (b) residual risk profile after one year, (c) vendor-relationship and contractual changes, and (d) implementation timeline"); the from-binaries run regressed to a deterministic-prefix paragraph echo ("Minimally satisfy: Team, this is the CIO..."). On SVB the inverse happened: the web_fetch run emitted the deterministic-prefix shape, while the from-binaries run distilled cleanly. The user-facing request paragraph was identical across both extraction paths; only the chunks fed to the Procedure-role retriever differed. The Interpreter is not domain-bound; it has two output modes, and chunk shape selects between them.

**3. The Validation role's gap-acknowledgment is content-tripwire, not constraint-count driven.** The web_fetch CrowdStrike validator rationale explicitly flagged the anchor-vs-COA-count mismatch ("potential insufficiency in delivering three distinct strategic options"). The from-binaries CrowdStrike validator dropped that specificity on the same input scenario with only the upstream chunks different. Validation can detect the gap, does not always notice, and cannot fix it from its pipeline position even when it does.

**4. The Binding role refuses to bind contradictory inputs rather than fabricating around them.** On the SVB from-binaries run, the `mandate-binding` model emitted three consecutive clean JSON responses of the shape `{"error": "<text>"}` — each one explaining, in natural language, that one COA cannot bind to a three-option anchor minimum. This is the same Decomposition single-COA contradiction surfacing one step downstream. The Binding fine-tune is well-trained: it is honest about input contradictions rather than papering over them. The behavior is probabilistic at temperature 0.1: HANDOFF_16c showed 3 of 3 refusals on direct Ollama calls against a frozen captured prompt; HANDOFF_17c showed 1 of 7 refusals across full-pipeline runs (where Decomposition output content and RAG chunk selection vary stochastically). The contradiction is always present in principle; Binding notices it when the upstream pipeline assembles the inputs in a particular shape.

## Apparatus patch (v2 candidate)

A side-loaded patch on `feature/binding-refusal-as-gap-sideload` extends the apparatus to detect Binding's structured refusals and route them to `output.gap_reports` instead of treating them as parse failures. The AEGIS-eval test suite passes on the branch (1443 passed, 27 skipped) including five new refusal-specific cases. The patch fires correctly under refusal (deterministic stub-adapter replay of the captured payload, in HANDOFF_17c Lane 3) and does not over-fire on non-refusal scenarios (Volt and CrowdStrike v2 sanity, in Lane 1). The project's main branch is unchanged. The frozen v1 tree is unchanged. The patch is ready to migrate to upstream AEGIS (Autonomous Engineering Governance and Intelligence System) as the candidate tag `mandate-eval-primary-2026q2-v2-candidate-binding-refusal` once `~/Desktop/AEGIS` is clean.

## Direct baseline comparison

A single-prompt Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Baseline 1, no RAG, no role decomposition) was run on the CrowdStrike request. Its anchor was markedly denser than MANDATE-primary's: 9 structured minimum entries vs MANDATE's 1 free-text paragraph; 5 structured target entries vs 1; 6 machine-readable constraints (one literally encoding `options_count == 3`) vs 0; 8 explicitly-named suspected gaps vs none. Baseline 1 does not produce courses_of_action (no Decomposition role), so the multi-COA question cannot be answered on the B1 side. Cost differential is $0.033 per call vs zero (local Ollama). The structural-detail differential is large and is exactly what the formal Phase 6 study's O1 anchor-completeness outcome will quantify against SME ground truth.

## Implications for the formal Phase 6 study

The five pre-registered primary outcomes operationalize the findings directly. O1 will quantify the anchor-completeness gap against SME ground truth on 120 main-corpus tasks; the three-judge ensemble grades against ground truth, not against subjective preference. O2a/O2b will measure gap-detection recall on the gap-triggering subset (where MANDATE's mandate-as-code schema's gap-report path is strictly more expressive than B1's `suspected_gaps` field, and where the v2 candidate patch would surface Binding refusals as additional gap evidence on v2-evaluated runs). O3 counts fabrications. O4 measures schema validity. O5 measures adversarial resistance under prompt injection. Whether the Decomposition single-COA structure plus the cryptographic trace chain plus the gap-report path outweighs the apparent anchor-density gap on the comparable dimensions is the substantive question Phase 6 will answer. The demo work above does not pre-judge that question; it confirms the apparatus is verified end-to-end on real, source-grounded scenarios, and it documents the cross-role content-sensitivity that the formal data will quantify.
